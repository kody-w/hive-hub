# Desk caddy dry-fit and inspection planning

SYNTHETIC operating reference. No parts exist as a result of this seed.
This document deliberately does not instruct fabrication, machine operation,
material processing, or purchased-component installation.

## Two-part assembly concept

After separately approved design, material, process, and physical prototype
reviews, the intended object would consist of a tray and a removable insert.
The insert's two long rails run along the inner front/back edges; its two
crossbars create three bays. It rests on the tray floor, with a nominal
1.5 mm gap to each side and its top below the rim. No adhesive, fastener,
snap feature, or pressure fit is intended.

## Prospective fit-check sequence

1. Identify a tray/insert pair and confirm both belong to the same design
   revision. Never combine nominal-180 and proposed-196 drawings.
2. Record outer width, depth, and height using the inspection sheet. Record
   actual readings only if a separately approved physical sample exists.
3. Measure the inner width and insert width. Calculate
   `(inner_width - insert_width) / 2`; target 1.5 mm, reference range 1–2 mm.
   This scalar check does not prove squareness or front/back clearance.
4. Review visible edges and overall condition; any unresolved defect holds
   the pair. The authored `edge_pass` column is only an exercise fixture.
5. A later approved dry-fit trial should allow insertion/removal without
   forcing, show no obvious rocking on a flat desk, and retain loose dry
   stationery within the bays. Record binding and instability as failures.
6. Segregate any mismatched/nonconforming pair for design review. Do not infer
   a safe correction process from this document.
7. Pack only after the applicable review gates. A mathematical packing pass
   does not validate transit protection, load limits, or real shipping.

The synthetic samples intentionally test only selected dimensions and an
authored edge flag. They do not establish sampling confidence, manufacturing
capability, durability, material suitability, or a completed batch.
