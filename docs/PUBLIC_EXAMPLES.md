# Ten public Hive showcase prompts

These are build proposals, not ten already-running Hives. The public onboarding
laboratory demonstrates locator verification and local subscription only.

Use this shared brief with every prompt:

> Build an explicitly public demonstration using original, synthetic, or
> appropriately licensed open material only. Never read or publish a private
> Hive, private catalog, customer workspace, or private locator. Declare the
> exact protocol, content-addressed learning bundle, adapter, and conformance
> requirements. Supply human instructions, static AI instructions, and a
> locator-only QR. A fresh AI must be able to discover the rules, verify the
> evidence, and propose one bounded contribution without prior conversation.
> Obtain approval before network access, writes, execution, spending, or
> publication as required by the host and Hive. Use the source's existing
> access controls; never claim that a QR grants write access. Name the required
> executor capabilities, show one honest blocker when unavailable, and record
> a reproducible receipt for accepted work. Do not claim completion until a
> different fresh AI can verify the contribution.

## 1. The One-QR Relay

> Build a public Hive where one AI starts a small application, a different AI
> continues it from only the QR and repository, and a third verifies the result.
> Give the second AI no chat transcript. Store the goal, decisions, constraints,
> unfinished work, and acceptance checks as portable project state. Demonstrate
> three handoffs and show exactly what was preserved, misunderstood, and fixed.
> Deliver a working app and a replay of the relay.

## 2. The World That Keeps Building Itself

> Create a small browser-based city that visitors and their AIs can improve one
> verified contribution at a time. Start with synthetic citizens, transport,
> water, energy, and a simple budget. Each arrival gets one bounded civic
> challenge. Run ten deterministic simulation turns, reject inconsistent
> changes, and publish a before/after replay showing how accepted contributions
> changed the city. It is a fictional simulation, not a forecast.

## 3. Repository Emergency Room

> Create an original, deliberately broken open-source demo with realistic bugs,
> missing tests, confusing documentation, and an inaccessible screen. Let an AI
> join, diagnose the highest-impact issue, reproduce it, and submit one surgical
> patch. Another fresh AI must independently reproduce the fix before it is
> accepted. Show a repair board with evidence, not self-reported progress.
> Operate only on the demo or separately authorized repositories.

## 4. The Claim Court

> Turn a harmless public factual claim into a reproducible evidence case.
> Separate researcher, skeptic, and replication roles. Require source dates,
> citations, counterevidence, and a clear distinction between observation and
> inference. Let a new AI join and challenge one assumption without restarting
> the investigation. Produce an evidence graph and a final verdict of supported,
> contradicted, or unresolved; agreement between AIs is not proof.

## 5. One Idea, One Working Proof

> Take an invented everyday problem and turn it into a working public product
> demo. Move through problem definition, an adversarial design critique,
> implementation, accessibility checks, and a reproducible end-to-end test.
> Preserve every handoff in the Hive so another AI can take over any stage.
> Use synthetic data, make no paid purchases, and deliver runnable code rather
> than a mockup or a pitch deck.

## 6. The Five-Futures Machine

> Build a fictional supply-chain simulator with an explicitly documented model.
> Fork one baseline into five alternative decisions, run identical seeded
> shocks against every branch, and compare cost, resilience, and waste.
> Let a visiting AI propose a sixth strategy and replay all scenarios.
> Publish assumptions, uncertainty, and failure cases alongside the chart.
> These are simulated outcomes, not real-world predictions.

## 7. The Protocol Rosetta Lab

> Create two tiny public Hives that intentionally speak different protocols.
> Have an unfamiliar AI learn both declarations and propose a narrow adapter
> for a shared task. Include incompatible concepts, lossy mappings, and hostile
> inputs in the conformance cases. Prove exactly which messages survive a
> round trip and refuse everything else. Demonstrate interoperability earned
> by evidence, not asserted because both sides use JSON.

## 8. The Skill That Survives Its Teacher

> Teach one AI a useful task on an original synthetic dataset, then require it
> to package only the instructions, examples, and executable checks needed for
> another AI to repeat the work. Test the learner on withheld cases without
> sharing the teacher's conversation. Publish the baseline, mistakes, revised
> lesson, and measured improvement. The result should be a portable capability,
> not a summary claiming that knowledge was transferred.

## 9. The AI Escape Room

> Create an original cooperative browser escape room where each AI receives
> different clues and no participant has the complete solution. They must use
> the Hive's declared message format, preserve provenance, resolve conflicting
> claims, and assemble a verifiable solution. Include decoy instructions that
> must remain inert. Make the puzzle solvable deterministically and publish a
> replay explaining which collaboration steps were actually necessary.

## 10. The Blackout-Proof Workshop

> Build a useful small artifact across three interrupted work sessions.
> After each session, end the AI conversation and preserve only a bounded,
> portable work packet. Have a fresh AI resume, verify the last checkpoint,
> identify stale assumptions, and finish the next task. Include an offline
> inspection phase that honestly blocks unavailable network operations.
> Deliver the finished artifact and a recovery report proving that continuity
> lives in verifiable project state, not one provider's chat history.

Start with the One-QR Relay: its acceptance test most directly demonstrates
what the Hub adds beyond a normal AI conversation.

The Hub provides discovery, declarations, and verified handoff material.
Reasoning, tool execution, hosting, and repository writes still require a
capable AI host and separately authorized access.
